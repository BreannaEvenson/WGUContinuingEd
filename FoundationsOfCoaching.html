Step One

Foundations of Coaching Workshop

To prepare for the Foundations of Coaching workshop, review the information on this page. This information should be reviewed before the workshop so you are able to fully participate with the group. Each Foundations of Coaching workshop will consist of a group of 2-5 coaches, real student scenarios, and opportunities to think critically and have conversations about the four foundational coaching skills listed below.

Active Listening

Active listening means being fully present, focusing on both the speaker’s words and nonverbal cues, and responding in a way that shows understanding. It includes behaviors like maintaining eye contact, removing distractions, allowing the speaker to finish, using small affirmations, paraphrasing or summarizing, and asking clarifying or exploratory questions. The goal is to create a safe space and better understand the speaker’s message and challenges.

Powerful Questions

Powerful questions are open-ended and thought-provoking, designed to encourage reflection, deeper understanding, and self-discovery. They often begin with words like what, how, or why and explore underlying beliefs, motivations, and learning processes. Rather than giving students solutions, powerful questions help students think through challenges and discover their own answers.

Encouragement &
Meaningful Feedback

Encouragement and meaningful feedback help students recognize their strengths while identifying clear areas for growth. Effective feedback combines empathy, clarity, and constructive guidance, while also being specific and actionable by referring to particular behaviors and offering concrete suggestions. It should also highlight progress so students can see what is working and continue building on it.

Goal-Setting

Goal setting is about turning broad intentions into clear, manageable steps that give students something concrete to work toward. Strong goals are more specific than vague statements like “I want to do better” and help clarify what the student plans to accomplish, how they’ll know they’re making progress, and when they’ll take action. In coaching conversations, the goal is to help students leave with a realistic next step they can actually follow through on.

Do not move onto the Foundational Coaching Skills simulation until you have attended the Foundations of Coaching workshop.

Step Three

Self-Evaluation

For your self-evaluation, you will request a call or live event recording and listen for the coaching skills that were covered in this learning path. While you are listening to your recording, fill out the form on this page and submit your responses. Note: The recording must be from an interaction that was performed after you attended the Foundations of Coaching workshop and completed the Foundational Coaching Skills simulation exercise. Therefore, it may take a few days for the development supervisor to provide your recording. If you are a live event coach only, one of your live events will be recorded for you to review.

Self-Evaluation Reflections

Overall, how effective do you feel this coaching interaction was? (1 = not very effective, 5 = very effective)
How consistently did you demonstrate active listening throughout the appointment or live event? (1 = rarely, 5 = consistently)
How effectively did you use open-ended, thought-provoking questions? (1 = rarely, 5 = very effectively)
How effectively did you recognize the student’s strengths, progress, or effort? (1 = rarely, 5 = very effectively)
When you provided feedback or guidance, how specific and actionable was it? (1 = very vague, 5 = very specific and actionable)
Did the student leave the appointment or live event with a clear next step or goal?
