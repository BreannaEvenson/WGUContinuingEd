Learning Objectives

Throughout this learning path, you should gain these competencies.

Model a study strategy during the I Do stage while keeping focus on the process rather than the course content.
Use questions, prompts, and wait time during the We Do to support the student without taking over.
Create a clear transition from the We Do to the You Do.
Demonstrate a complete I Do, We Do, You Do sequence.
STEP ONE

You're the Student

Experience I Do, We Do, You Do from the student's perspective. A development program Senior Peer Navigator will coach you on a real challenge in a course you are currently working on.

  1. Before scheduling: Identify your current course, the specific topic or module, and the challenge you want help working through. Have the relevant course material ready.
  2. Go to the Training and Development page in SharePoint and use one of the development program Senior Peer Navigators' booking links.
  3. Immediately after booking: Send that Senior Peer Navigator a Teams message with the meeting day and time, your course, and the specific topic or module you want to work through. Include your challenge so they can prepare.
  4. Attend as the student. Bring your actual materials and participate in the practice.

STEP TWO

Shadow & Reflect

Shadow two appointments of your choice and one live event. Complete a separate reflection for each. You do not need to find a perfect example of the framework: noticing an effective move, a missed opportunity, or a stage that did not occur can all support your learning.

STEP THREE

From We Do to You Do

In We Do, the student practices with your support. In You Do, the student takes responsibility for using the strategy. Your transition should make the task, level of support, and next step clear. You Do can happen during a session or continue afterward with a specific plan.

Make the shift visible

Give the student a manageable piece of material and explain what they will do independently. During independent practice, allow them to make decisions and finish before offering feedback. A pause or moment of uncertainty is not automatically a reason to step in. If they need support, use a brief prompt and let them resume the work.

In appointments

When there is time to practice

“So far, I've demonstrated the strategy, and you've gotten in some really strong practice. Now I want you to try this next step on your own. Talk me through your thought process as you go.”

Identify the next question, paragraph, concept, or other small task. Listen to the student's thinking without completing it for them. Once they finish, invite their reflection and provide supportive, constructive feedback on the process.

When time is limited

Help the student describe exactly how they will use the strategy after the appointment. Ask:

  • Which part of your course material will you try next?
  • When will you work on it?
  • What will tell you that the strategy is helping?
  • What will you do if you get stuck?

Have them explain the plan in their own words. Arrange a follow-up before ending the appointment when appropriate.

In live events

When there is time to practice

“We've all had a chance to practice this strategy together. Now we're going to take five minutes to try the next part independently. Use the next two concepts in this module to create your own flashcards.”

Specify the course material, the task, the time available, and how participants will share afterward. Clear instructions make it easier to engage. Allow independent study time, then debrief:

  • How did that feel?
  • What was challenging?
  • What went well?
  • What came up that was unexpected?

When time is limited

Ask participants to identify a specific next use of the strategy. Invite them to share the topic, when they will try it, how they will check its usefulness, and what they will do if they need support.

Feedback that helps the student improve

Describe what you noticed, connect it to the strategy, and offer an opportunity to adjust. Keep feedback focused on how the student used the strategy.

If a student copies the textbook word for word

“I noticed you used the same wording as the textbook. How could you put that idea into your own words so the flashcard helps you check your understanding?”

Give the student time to revise. Then ask what changed and how the revision might support studying.

If the student catches an overloaded flashcard

“You noticed that the card was starting to include several ideas and decided to split it. That keeps each card focused on one thing to recall. What helped you recognize that?”

Name the useful action so the student knows what to repeat.

Check your understanding

Choose an answer for each scenario, then check your responses.

STEP FOUR

Coaching Circle

Practice a complete I Do, We Do, You Do sequence in a supportive setting. Request your Coaching Circle by clicking on the request button below.

What to expect

  • Three participants: two coaches currently completing this learning path and either a Senior Peer Navigator or the development program supervisor.
  • Three rotating responsibilities: coach, student, and observer. Each person takes each role once.
  • Short practice: each coaching session lasts about 10 minutes. After each session, share brief feedback and rotate roles.
  • Materials are provided: scenarios, course material, and selected study strategies will be supplied during the meeting. All coaching circles will use general education courses.
  • Your focus: model the strategy, guide practice without taking over, make a clear transition, and give the student an opportunity to use the strategy independently.
