Learning objectives

Make room for what happens next

Moving Forward is an intentional piece of AFFIRM. This learning path builds on your onboarding and Foundations of Coaching workshop, then extends those skills into ongoing accountability appointments.

  • Understand the value of Moving Forward as an intentional piece of the AFFIRM coaching model.
  • Use flexible pacing to preserve approximately five minutes for Moving Forward in appointments and live events.
  • Use powerful questions to help students develop meaningful, realistic, short-term goals.
  • Facilitate accountability appointments through all six steps of AFFIRM.
  • Maintain a coaching mindset during accountability conversations.

The difference is in the follow-through

Both conversations begin with the same student intention: stick to a study plan and get through a good amount of material. The difference is what the coach helps the student do with that intention.

In the rushed conversation

The coach affirms the goal and quickly closes the call. “A good amount” remains undefined, and the student does not explore when they will study or what could interrupt their plan. Encouragement is present, but the student leaves with much of the planning still to do.

In the intentional conversation

The coach asks what the student means, and the student identifies two modules and a timeframe. A question about obstacles brings the football games into the conversation. The student chooses morning study time as an adjustment and identifies a day off as something to look forward to.

The effective conversation makes space for the student to think. The coach does not choose the goal or tell the student to skip the games. Instead, follow-up questions help the student develop a plan that fits their priorities. Asking how the plan feels also gives the student an opportunity to consider whether it is doable.

This example offers a useful starting point. Depending on the student, you could also ask what “complete” means, which mornings they will use, how they will notice progress, or what support they need. Let their answers guide your next question instead of treating goal setting as a script to recite.

Make the next step the student’s own.

Invite the student to state their plan in their own words. Listen for a meaningful action, a realistic timeframe, and a way to support follow-through. A thoughtful goal-setting conversation gives the student something concrete to act on after the session.

Make time for Moving Forward

Available for review

Sometimes Moving Forward is rushed because the earlier conversation uses all the available time. Instruction and practice can feel so productive that it is easy to keep going—until only a quick goodbye remains.

Plan for goal setting from the start. The timing below is a recommended guide for pacing AFFIRM, not a rule for ending every section at an exact minute. Respond to the student’s needs while keeping enough space for them to decide how they will use what they learned.

Recommended AFFIRM timing for appointments and live events
AFFIRM sectionAppointmentLive event
Achievement Orientation2 minutes5 minutes
Frame the Session4 minutes8 minutes
Focus on the Strategy6 minutes10 minutes
Intentional Practice9 minutes25 minutes
Review and Restate4 minutes7 minutes
Moving Forward5 minutes5 minutes
Total30 minutes60 minutes

Use time as a cue

You do not need to let the clock take over your thoughts. Checking the time between sections of AFFIRM can help you decide whether to narrow an example, finish the current practice attempt, or move into reflection. As you approach the final five minutes, transition intentionally into Moving Forward.

A transition you could use

“You’ve identified what helped during that practice. We have about five minutes left, and I want to use that time to talk about how you’d like to carry this into your studying. What would be a meaningful next step for you?”

In live events, preserve time for every student to identify a next step. Give a short period for individual thinking or writing, then invite a few students to share. Students can still create meaningful goals when there is not time to hear every plan aloud.

The priority is an intentional, unhurried goal-setting conversation. Be flexible with the earlier sections while protecting this opportunity for the student to plan.

04
Watch and reflect

Accountability through AFFIRM

Available for review

Watch a weekly accountability appointment. The student has made some progress and wants help following through. Notice how the coach uses questions, a targeted planning approach, and verbal practice to support the student through all six steps of AFFIRM.

What makes this an accountability coaching conversation?

The student arrives concerned about an unfinished goal. The coach uses that update as the beginning of an exploration: what happened, what the student learned, and how they can use that information to move forward.

  1. Achievement Orientation makes progress visible. The coach asks what it took to finish Lesson 3. The student recognizes both returning to study on Saturday and revisiting misunderstood terms. These details become useful information about how the student learns and follows through.
  2. Frame the session allows for deeper exploration of the student’s needs. The coach asks what would make the conversation useful and explores what “more disciplined” means. Together, they focus on developing a plan the student can realistically use.
  3. Focus on the Strategy introduces an approach that fits the need. After comparing Wednesday evening with Saturday morning, the coach introduces Energy-Based Planning. The discussion connects the approach to the student’s experience: available time and usable energy are both part of planning.
  4. Intentional Practice happens verbally. The student walks through Tuesday morning, identifies a stopping time, and considers work competing for the same block. They explore alternatives and decide what fits. Talking through an actual situation gives them practice adapting the approach to their life.
  5. Review and Restate brings the learning together. The student identifies a pattern: scheduling low-energy time and making missed study blocks even larger increased resistance. They use their earlier success to recognize that shorter, focused blocks can be productive.
  6. Moving Forward turns insight into a commitment. The student chooses Lesson 4 and the practice questions by Saturday, describes how Cornell notes will support their studying, considers how realistic the goal feels, and asks for another appointment. The coach helps connect the goal to what matters to the student.
Keep asking questions that help the student think.

“What helped?” “What got in the way?” and “What do you want to adjust?” can open useful exploration whether the student met, partially met, or did not meet their goal. Listen to the answer and build from it.

The student already has a study strategy they like. The coach responds to the current challenge—following through—by introducing a planning approach. Accountability appointments do not always need a new study technique, but they still need a clear purpose, active student participation, and meaningful next steps.

Carry the accountability plan into the week

The follow-up appointment gives the student a place to revisit progress. You can also invite them to choose how they will track progress between meetings and when they will check whether the plan needs adjustment. These questions extend the plan while keeping ownership with the student.
