Learning Objectives

Throughout this learning path, you should gain these competencies.

Active Listening: Understand what active listening is and demonstrate effective listening.
Powerful Questions: Identify the difference between deeply powerful, moderately powerful, and not powerful questions.
Encouragement & Meaningful Feedback: Can provide appropriate encouragement and meaningful feedback from a coaching perspective.
Goal-Setting: Demonstrates ability to facilitate a meaningful goal-setting conversation.

Step One

Foundations of Coaching Workshop

To prepare for the Foundations of Coaching workshop, review the information on this page. This information should be reviewed before the workshop so you are able to fully participate with the group.

Each Foundations of Coaching workshop will consist of a group of 2-5 coaches, real student scenarios, and opportunities to think critically and have conversations about the four foundational coaching skills listed below.

Active Listening

Active listening means being fully present, focusing on both the speaker's words and nonverbal cues, and responding in a way that shows understanding. It includes behaviors like maintaining eye contact, removing distractions, allowing the speaker to finish, using small affirmations, paraphrasing or summarizing, and asking clarifying or exploratory questions. The goal is to create a safe space and better understand the speaker's message and challenges.

Powerful Questions

Powerful questions are open-ended and thought-provoking, designed to encourage reflection, deeper understanding, and self-discovery. They often begin with words like what, how, or why and explore underlying beliefs, motivations, and learning processes. Rather than giving students solutions, powerful questions help students think through challenges and discover their own answers.

Encouragement &
Meaningful Feedback

Encouragement and meaningful feedback help students recognize their strengths while identifying clear areas for growth. Effective feedback combines empathy, clarity, and constructive guidance, while also being specific and actionable by referring to particular behaviors and offering concrete suggestions. It should also highlight progress so students can see what is working and continue building on it.

Goal-Setting

Goal setting is about turning broad intentions into clear, manageable steps that give students something concrete to work toward. Strong goals are more specific than vague statements like "I want to do better" and help clarify what the student plans to accomplish, how they'll know they're making progress, and when they'll take action. In coaching conversations, the goal is to help students leave with a realistic next step they can actually follow through on.
