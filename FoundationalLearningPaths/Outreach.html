Resource Matching vs. Resource Dumping

When we connect students to resources, the goal is to recommend the right support for what the student actually needs. It can be easy to fall back on a familiar “go-to” resource, especially when we know that resource is helpful. But a resource that works well for one student may not address the challenge another student is experiencing.

Strong outreach requires us to listen closely to what the student is telling us before deciding what to recommend. A student who is confused by a specific course concept may need a Course Instructor resource, while a student who understands the content but is struggling with how to study it may benefit more from Peer Coaching. The more clearly we understand the need, the more intentionally we can match the student to the support that is most likely to help.

Matching resources thoughtfully also helps prevent resource dumping — giving a student several links, services, or suggestions all at once in hopes that something will be useful. More information is not always more helpful. Too many options can feel overwhelming and may make it less likely that the student uses any of them. Instead, focus on one or two resources that directly connect to what the student shared, briefly explain why you think they could help, and make the next step easy to understand.

The goal is not to tell the student about everything WGU has available. The goal is to listen, identify the need, and connect the student with support that makes sense for them.
